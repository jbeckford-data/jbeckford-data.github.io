An Investigative Look Into a 0DTE SPX Strategy: Why Initial Results Can Mislead

By John Beckford • Dec 12, 2025

This analysis reconciles two independent backtest sources for a 0-Days-to-Expiration (0DTE) SPX strategy, applies conservative corrections, and quantifies the impact of commissions and early-sample trade timing on final performance.

QC Original: An Attractive First Pass

Over roughly three years and 807 trading days, the QC Original backtest displayed highly favorable statistics: a 93.68% win rate, a Sharpe ratio of 2.05, and a compounded annual return of 43.18%, all while reporting only an 11.31% maximum drawdown. From a surface-level, risk-adjusted perspective, this curve looks exceptionally strong—enough to make traders pay close attention.

OA Original: Independent Verification Reveals Differences

Independent verification against the OA Original dataset (815 days) produced materially different results. The OA backtest still delivered a high win rate, but the compounded annual return fell to 24.04%, the Sharpe ratio dropped to 0.72, and maximum drawdown expanded to 26.19%. Losses were larger on average (≈ -3.94%), calling attention to differences in fills, timestamps, or trade-exit logic between data sources.

Reconciliation Steps: Early Trades and Worst-Case Adjustments

The strategy’s edge depends heavily on the sequence of many small wins and occasional larger losses, which means that early-sample trade timing has outsized impact. For reconciliation, QC trades that exited earlier than OA’s matching exits were updated to OA’s later exit when appropriate. As a conservative stress test, any QC early winners that conflicted with OA early losers were changed to take OA’s losing result. This produces a pessimistic, but plausible, alternate history that guards against optimistic sequencing assumptions.

Commissions: An Underappreciated Drag

Both original datasets assumed zero transaction costs. After applying realistic per-contract commissions across the samples, total fees exceeded $8,138 across the backtest horizon. For a system with many small wins and fewer, larger losses, commissions compress net returns and extend recovery time after drawdowns.

QC Updated & OA Updated: The Adjusted Reality

After corrections and commissions, the updated datasets paint a far more conservative picture. QC Updated and OA Updated show compounded annual returns of 17.80% and 15.72%, respectively. Drawdowns increase to the low-30% range, and Sharpe ratios decline to 0.42 and 0.34. The strategy remains profitable but is markedly more sensitive to sequencing, costs, and data-source assumptions.

Conclusion

The initial QC backtest suggested near-perfect stability, but reconciliation with OA, conservative early-trade adjustments, and realistic commission assumptions revealed a far more modest outcome. The strategy remains profitable but shows increased drawdowns and weaker risk-adjusted returns. The broader lesson: treat exceptional backtests with skepticism and require multi-source validation and conservative assumptions before taking a strategy live.
