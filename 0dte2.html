An Investigative Look Into a 0DTE SPX Strategy

Why initial backtest results can mislead — QC vs OA reconciled, commissions applied, and worst-case adjustments shown

Quant Research Options / 0DTE Backtest Validation Equity Curves

Introduction

Over the last several years, interest in 0-Days-to-Expiration (0DTE) SPX strategies has grown rapidly. The strategy examined here spans roughly three years and, at first glance, appears to deliver exceptional results: a high win rate, strong Sharpe, and a very high compounded annual return. This article shows how independent verification, conservative reconciliation, and adding realistic trading costs materially change that initial picture.

QC Original — A Compelling First Pass

The QC Original backtest reports 807 trading days with strong metrics: 93.68% win rate, Sharpe 2.05, and a 43.18% compounded annual return while showing only an 11.31% max drawdown. At first glance this looks like a robust edge—but that conclusion requires independent verification.

OA Original — Independent Verification

Testing the same strategy against a second source (OA Original, 815 days) produced materially different results. OA shows a 24.04% annual return, a Sharpe of 0.72, and a 26.19% maximum drawdown. These differences point to variations in fills, timestamps, or trade-exit logic between the two sources.

Reconciliation — Early Trades & Worst-Case Adjustments

Because the strategy’s returns are driven by many small wins and occasional larger losses, early-sample trade timing is critical. To be conservative, QC trades that exited before OA’s matching exits were aligned to OA when appropriate. Where QC early winners conflicted with OA early losers, QC was updated to reflect OA’s worse outcome—creating a pessimistic but plausible alternate history.

Commissions Applied

Neither original dataset included transaction costs. After applying realistic per-contract commissions across the samples, total fees exceeded $8,138. For a strategy with many small wins, these fees materially reduced compounded returns and extended drawdown recovery times.

QC Updated & OA Updated — The Adjusted View

With reconciliation and commissions applied, QC Updated and OA Updated show much more modest outcomes: compounded annual returns of 17.80% and 15.72%, drawdowns around 32–34%, and Sharpe ratios closer to 0.4. The system remains profitable but is more sensitive to sequencing and costs than the original QC numbers suggested.

Conclusion

The original QC backtest suggested near-perfect stability, but reconciliation with OA, conservative early-trade adjustments, and realistic commission assumptions revealed a far more modest outcome. The strategy remains profitable but shows greater drawdowns, higher volatility, and lower risk-adjusted returns. The takeaway: require multi-source validation and conservative assumptions before deploying a strategy live.
