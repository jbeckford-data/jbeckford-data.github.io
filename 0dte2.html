An Investigative Look Into a 0DTE SPX Strategy: Why Initial Results Can Mislead

By John Beckford • Dec 12, 2025

Independent validation and reconciliation of two backtest sources for a 0-Days-to-Expiration (0DTE) SPX strategy. This article compares the original QC and OA outputs, documents reconciliation and commission adjustments, and presents the corrected performance picture.

QC Original — A Compelling First Pass

Over roughly three years and 807 trading days, the QC Original backtest displayed favorable statistics: a 93.68% win rate, Sharpe ratio of 2.05, and a 43.18% compounded annual return, while reporting only an 11.31% maximum drawdown. At face value, this profile looks exceptionally strong.

OA Original — Independent Verification

Verification against the OA Original dataset (815 days) revealed materially different results. OA reported a 24.04% annual return, a Sharpe of 0.72, and a 26.19% drawdown. These differences highlight potential variances in fills, timestamps, or exit logic between sources.

Reconciliation and Worst-Case Adjustments

Because the strategy depends on a long series of small wins and a few large losses, early-sample timing is critically important. QC exits that occurred earlier than OA’s were updated in QC to match OA when appropriate; QC early winners that contradicted OA early losers were conservatively replaced with OA’s losing outcomes to create a plausible worst-case scenario.

Commissions Applied

Neither original dataset included transaction costs. When realistic per-contract commissions were added, total fees exceeded $8,138 across the sample. For a strategy built on many small wins, these fees materially reduced compounded returns and lengthened recovery times from drawdowns.

QC Updated & OA Updated — The Adjusted View

After reconciliation and fees, QC Updated and OA Updated show more modest performance: compounded annual returns of 17.80% and 15.72%, respectively, with drawdowns in the low-30% range and Sharpe ratios down near 0.4. The strategy remains profitable but is noticeably more sensitive to sequencing and costs.

Conclusion

The initial QC backtest suggested near-perfect stability, but reconciliation with OA, conservative early-trade adjustments, and realistic commission assumptions revealed a much more modest outcome. The strategy remains profitable, but with greater drawdowns, higher volatility, and lower risk-adjusted returns. The takeaway: require multi-source validation and conservative assumptions before deploying a strategy live.
